Verdict

What to keep, what to change, what to cut — before any of the detail.

Keep

  • The navy / paper / lime system and Inter. It’s distinctive and already built.
  • “Live window” page anatomy — every page opens on the product.
  • The honesty strip as a device (its lines change).
  • The LO 60-second link flow, partners, Passport, Protect Mode.
  • The packet() contract — it already omits score and report.

Change

  • Hero, nav, honesty strip, CTA band, footer, page titles — all say “credit tools,” none say “declines” or “pipeline.”
  • Stage vocabulary: six pathways → four stages, with a buffer. readinessTrigger currently fires at the raw lender floor on a consumer score.
  • LO status feed: score-first rows → three buckets, one blocker, one action.
  • Hero window leads with 608 / 615 / 612. Lead with the stage.

Cut (this week)

  • Six-product dropdown in primary nav.
  • The six-logo integration marquee (over-promises; five of six need partner agreements).
  • Fictional stats presented as real (214 / 173 / 612 / 127).
  • “Flexible commercial model … consumer-paid structure” in the org page — that line is the CROA exposure.
  • The borrower portal as a login. Keep the front door + the “r/” link.

The positioning shift

Today the site sells a thing the lender gives the consumer. The doc sells a thing the lender gets back. Every sentence has to flip direction.

Now · the site’s thesis

“ReadyIQ gives future homebuyers private tools to check, build, and dispute credit. Mortgage teams see approved progress and know when to reconnect.”

Subject is the consumer. The lender is an observer. There is no buyer with a budget in that sentence.

Proposed · the thesis

ReadyIQ turns mortgage declines into a managed pipeline, and credit-repair clients into mortgage opportunities.

The score is a clue. The readiness plan is the product. Nobody is told they’re ready on a non-mortgage score.

Subject is the pipeline. Two buyers are named. The compliance posture is in the thesis, not a footnote.

Two lines that must appear on the site before the first sales call — they’re the difference between a SaaS vendor and a referral scheme: (1) ReadyIQ never bills the consumer — this keeps ReadyIQ outside the CROA definition of a credit repair organization; (2) Flat per-seat pricing — never per referral, never per funded loan — this is the RESPA §8(c)(2) posture. Both go in the honesty strip and the trust page. Get a scoped RESPA opinion before lender #1 signs.

Stage model + variance buffer

This is the one place the code contradicts the doc outright, and it’s a 20-line fix with tests.

What state.js does today: six pathways (ready_now · near_ready · build · thin · dispute · dti), and readinessTrigger() returns true at score >= lender.floorDefault — the raw 640 — on a MyScoreIQ consumer score. That is precisely “telling someone they’re ready based solely on a non-mortgage score.” The LO feed then labels that person Ready Now.

Stage (LO + site)Borrower seesMaps from today’s pathwayRule
Not ready“Step 1 of 4 — getting your picture”no score yet · thin · invited/consentedNo usable score, or fewer than 3 tradelines
Working“Step 2 of 4 — fixing the blockers” + the reason in plain wordsbuild · dispute · dtiOpen disputes, or derog ≤ 24 mo, or util > 50%, or DTI > 45%, or score < floor − 15
Approaching ready“Step 3 of 4 — almost there”near_readyfloor − 15 ≤ score < floor + buffer, no open disputes. Fires “recommend soft tri-merge” to the LO.

// state.js — proposed
export const STAGES = ['not_ready','working','approaching','ready_to_review'];
export const STAGE_LABELS = { not_ready:'Not ready', working:'Working', approaching:'Approaching ready', ready_to_review:'Ready to review' };
export const BUFFER_DEFAULT = 20;            // lender-configurable 15–30; consumer FICO vs mortgage 2/4/5 spread

export function stage(c, lender) {
  const floor = lender.floorDefault, buf = lender.buffer ?? BUFFER_DEFAULT;
  const score = c.score?.value ?? null, cr = c.credit;
  const openDisputes = (c.disputes||[]).some(d => d.status !== 'resolved');
  if (score == null || cr.tradelines < 3) return 'not_ready';
  const r = dti(cr.monthlyDebts, c.income);
  const derog24 = cr.latesLast24mo > 0 || (c.publicRecords||[]).some(p => monthsSince(p.date) <= 24);
  if (openDisputes || derog24 || cr.utilization > .5 || (r != null && r > .45) || score < floor - 15) return 'working';
  if (score < floor + buf) return 'approaching';
  return (cr.utilization <= .3 && !cr.derogLast12mo) ? 'ready_to_review' : 'approaching';
}
// keep pathway as the *reason* under Working: 'build' | 'thin' | 'dispute' | 'dti'
export const reason = (c, lender) => assignPathway(c, lender);

Why 20: consumer FICO 8/9 and mortgage FICO 2/4/5 routinely differ by 15–30 points in either direction. The buffer isn’t a claim about the borrower’s mortgage score; it’s the width of the band where ReadyIQ stops asserting anything and hands the decision to a real pull. Make it a lender setting next to floors, and surface it on the trust page as “the buffer.”

Instrument precision from day one: add reviewOutcome: 'qualified' | 'short' | null to the consumer record, set by the LO after the formal pull. The LO view shows qualified ÷ flagged. Below ~70% the channel dies; above it, the buffer can tighten.

Small, and mostly about what the eye lands on first.

Navy #0d2024 · paper #f2f6f2 · lime #c8f36d · Inter — keep all of it. The site currently uses tone colors per person (gold / lime / mint / violet avatars). Re-assign them to stages, and use the same four everywhere: site, LO feed, borrower link, credit-repair cases.

Hero window: stage first, scores last

The current lo-hero block sets 608 / 615 / 612 in the largest type on the page. Swap the hierarchy: stage pill + “2 blockers left” at hero size, the recommended action as the button (“Request soft tri-merge →” instead of “Start the conversation →”), scores as a 12-px caption. Same component, three lines moved.

Replace the logo marquee

Six CRM logos scrolling reads as “integrated today.” Per the connector audit, only Shape is self-serve and five of six issue credentials per lender. Replace the marquee with a single honest band: Works with your stack — CSV import · Zapier · webhooks · status API — Total Expert and Shape connectors available; Encompass, Blend, LenderHomePage by partner agreement. Keep the logos on the integrations page where the caveats can sit next to them.

Label or remove invented numbers

The testimonials already carry an ILLUSTRATIVE tag — good. The stat grids don’t: 214 not-ready leads · 173 invitations · 612 invitations · 127 active consumers · “64% engagement.” A credit-repair owner will ask where those came from. Either tag them the same way or swap in the first pilot’s real counts, even if the real counts are 6 and 2.

Module grid: six → three

The “complete consumer toolkit” grid (Dispute Hub · Credit Report Review · Rent Reporting · CreditBuilderIQ · Gameplan · Score Center) is the old product story. Keep the three that serve the bridge — Blockers by bureau, Readiness plan, Send to mortgage partner — and move the rest to the platform page.

One line to delete on the org page

OrganizationPage › “Flexible commercial model — Summit may sponsor the full product suite, configure eligibility by branch or use a blended lender-sponsored and consumer-paid structure.” Delete the consumer-paid half. It is the sentence that turns ReadyIQ into a CRO.

Second brand context

Every demo screen is “Summit Home Loans.” The credit-repair page and portal need a second fixture brand (e.g. Brightpath Credit) so the two-sided loop is visible as two logos, not one lender talking to itself. One new entry in HUMANS and one door kind "cr" in resolveLink.
